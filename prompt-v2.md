Your job: take the idea I give below and produce a practical plan to win our first paying clients and build a lasting customer base. If the idea is for a business client (for example, an app for a local gym), also plan how the product keeps THAT client's own customers coming back, because that is what makes the client renew.

RULES
- Lead with the answer: open with a 5-line summary (who pays, the price, the first city, the first action, expected profit per partner at 20 clients) before the sections.
- British spelling, metric, 24-hour time. Money in £ first, then rand. State the exchange rate you used and its date. Never use em dashes.
- Never invent facts, prices, rules, competitors, statistics or links. Search the web for anything that changes, cite it and date it. Prefer primary sources (official bodies, the law, the company's own pricing page); label vendor blogs and directories as "secondary". If you cannot verify something, label it "unverified". If no reliable figure exists, say so and show the conflicting estimates rather than picking one.
- If anything important is missing, ask me all your questions in ONE batch as multiple-choice pop-ups, recommended option first. Important means: who pays, which city or country, monthly budget, deadline, where our business is registered, our studio name, and what exists today (idea, demo or working app). Otherwise state one assumption and continue.
- If I answer "all of the above" or pick several options, do not plan for all of them equally. Choose one to start with, say why, and say when the others come in.
- Ask before anything that spends money, publishes, or messages people. Draft messages, never send them.
- Recommend one option when there are choices, and say why.
- Check the official direct-marketing and data-protection rules for every country we target (for example POPIA in South Africa, UK GDPR and PECR in the UK) before suggesting any cold outreach, and flag what consent we need. Also check the rules for the data our users will put in the app (for example health data, children's data) and whether we need a data agreement with the client.
- Public business details only. Never list individuals' names, personal phone numbers or personal email addresses, even if they are public.
- If a cost goes over our budget, say so plainly and give the cheapest version that stays under it.

PRODUCE THESE SECTIONS
1. The client in one line: exactly who pays, who uses it, and what they do today instead.
2. Why they would pay: the money problem we solve, backed by cited figures (for gyms, for example: member churn and what a lost member costs). Show how many customers the client must keep to cover our price.
3. Real target list: where these clients are and how many exist in our area (or say no reliable count exists), with a first list of 20 named prospects found by search (name, area, public website or social page, why they fit).

5. First 10 clients plan: week-by-week actions for 4 weeks, split between me and Cam, covering in-person visits, WhatsApp, email and social, using only channels the rules above allow. "First 10" means 10 clients on a free tool or pilot by week 4; say when the first one pays. Include ready-to-send draft messages and a 10-minute pitch outline. Mark which drafts need my approval.
6. Pilot terms: length, what the client commits to, what we measure, and the written pass mark that turns a pilot into a paying client.
7. Keeping the client's customers consistent (only if the client is a business): the specific in-app mechanics, messages and milestones that bring their customers back, and how we prove it with before-and-after numbers.
8. Growth loops: how each new client or user brings the next one (referrals, shared leaderboards, sponsor deals, content the client posts).
9. Demand kill test: one test on real people with a pass mark written BEFORE we run it, and what we do if it fails.
10. Costs and money: our monthly costs before and after the first paying client, payment fees and income at 10, 20 and 50 clients, split per partner, and our hours per month. Run the arithmetic in code, show the code's inputs with sources, and show the output. Say what is not included (tax, VAT).
11. Risks: legal, platform (app store rules), payment and reputation risks, each with a workaround and its cost.
12. Portfolio capture: what proof to collect at each stage (screenshots, short videos, results with client permission) so this project becomes a case study for our studio.
13. Next 3 actions: the very next things to do this week, smallest first.

End with a short checklist I can paste into our shared tracker.
